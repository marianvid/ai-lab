# The two evaluation sets — where they come from

Both originate in **ParallaxVox**, Marian's news-analysis project, at
`/Volumes/Marian_Backup/work/parallaxvox/benchmark-data/gold/`. They were built
for that pipeline's own quality work, not for this study; using them here means
the tests measure something the project actually needs.

## `relevance_set.jsonl` — multilingual classification

**600 items**, one JSON object per line:

```json
{"url": "...", "title": "...", "desc": "...", "gold": true, "tld": "ru"}
```

**Origin.** A live pipeline run — fresh RSS collection plus a light fetch —
producing a pool of 4 172 real articles. Not synthetic, not sampled from an
existing dataset. The pool is `benchmark-data/gold/relevance/pool.json`; the
labels are `gold.json`.

**Labelling.** 600 of the 4 172 were labelled by `claude-opus`, medium effort, on
20 July 2026. The judgment is `desc_relevant`: is this article relevant to the
Ukraine–Russia war fault line, given its headline and description. 52% are
relevant. Earlier 120-item and 900-item pilots were deleted; this is the only
valid set.

**Composition.** By the publisher's top-level domain, a rough proxy for language:

| | .ru | .ua | .lt | .news | .com | .pl | .md |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| items | 154 | 117 | 114 | 99 | 95 | 11 | 10 |
| relevant | 105 | 65 | 18 | 53 | 65 | 6 | **0** |

Two things to carry forward. **Lithuanian is heavily unbalanced** — 18 relevant
of 114 — which makes its F1 sensitive and is exactly why it separates strong
models from weak ones. And **the Moldovan subset has no relevant items**, so F1
is undefined there and must be reported as such rather than as 0.000; those
articles are written in Russian in any case.

**Description text was truncated to 700 characters** when building this file.

## `translation_set.jsonl` — English into seven languages

**42 pairs**: 6 source articles × 7 target languages.

```json
{"slug": "a1_oilcap", "lang": "ro", "title": "...",
 "source": "<English article, Markdown>", "reference": "<reference translation>"}
```

**Sources.** Six English articles produced by the ParallaxVox pipeline itself,
2 165 to 5 240 characters each, on Ukraine–Russia war topics. They are the
project's own output, not third-party copy.

**References.** Generated by **`claude-fable-5`**. They are *reference
translations produced by a model*, not human translations, and any published
result must say so plainly — otherwise a chrF++ figure reads as agreement with a
human, which it is not.

**Languages.** Romanian, German, French, Spanish, Polish, Ukrainian, Russian.

**The original protocol used a different judge.** ParallaxVox scores these with
Gemini, deliberately chosen from a different model family than the one that wrote
the references, on a 0–10 rubric. This study used chrF++ instead: mechanical, no
judge model, no family bias, reproducible by anyone. The two are not comparable —
do not put a chrF++ number next to a rubric score.

## Copyright, for the public repository

**The labels are Marian's. The article text is not.**

`relevance_set.jsonl` carries headlines and description excerpts from Russian,
Ukrainian, Lithuanian, Polish, Moldovan and international news sites. Marian's
decision is to publish the corpus in full, with source attribution and an
explicit statement that it was collected for research.

What that requires in the published repository:

- Every item keeps its **source URL**, so attribution is per-item and verifiable.
- A clear statement of **purpose**: collected and redistributed for evaluating
  machine-translation and classification quality, not for redistribution as news.
- A **takedown contact**, and willingness to honour requests.
- No claim of ownership over the article text; the licence covers the labels,
  the harness and the results, not the quoted material.

`translation_set.jsonl` is cleaner: the sources are the project's own output and
the references are generated. Only the generated provenance needs stating.

## Rebuilding these files

Both are derived from the ParallaxVox gold directory by short scripts recorded in
this session. To rebuild `relevance_set.jsonl`: load `relevance/pool.json`, index
it by URL, join against `relevance/gold.json`, keep title, first 700 characters
of description, the `desc_relevant` label and the URL's top-level domain. To
rebuild `translation_set.jsonl`: for each `translation/sources/*.en.json`, pair
its body with every matching `translation/reference/<slug>.<lang>.md`.
